Meeting Reports (MIR)

What a Meeting Intelligence Report is, why every meeting needs one, and how to generate it — at the close of a meeting or for one that got missed.

The one thing to remember: every meeting ends with an MIR, and someone has to generate it. It doesn't happen on its own. The MIR is what turns a meeting into a record the rest of the system can use.

What an MIR is

An MIR — Meeting Intelligence Report — is a structured written summary of a meeting. Claude builds it from the meeting's recording, capturing every decision made, task assigned, blocker raised, and what the next meeting needs to focus on.

It does two jobs. First — for Thursday and ad hoc sessions — it gives the team a record to read through at the close: the meeting lead reads it aloud to confirm decisions, actions, and next steps before everyone leaves. (Stand-ups don't do this read-aloud — their MIR is generated after the meeting ends.) Second, for every meeting type, it becomes a permanent reference in #mir-reports that Claude reads when building the next agenda. (Channel details: the Slack guide.)

It doesn't generate itself

This is the most important thing about MIRs: someone has to trigger it. Claude does not produce an MIR automatically when a meeting ends. Claude can still read the raw Fireflies transcript later, so a meeting's content isn't lost without one — but the MIR is what makes the meeting usable by the rest of the system. Most importantly, the Monday stand-up's MIR is what sets Thursday's focus: no MIR means the Thursday agenda has nothing to pull from. It's also the record the team reads aloud at the close to confirm decisions, and the clean reference Claude reads instead of re-scanning a long transcript. Generating it is a fixed step, not an optional extra — after every stand-up, and at the close of every Thursday and ad hoc session.

Where it fits

The MIR is the hinge between one meeting and the next. A Monday stand-up's MIR sets the focus for Thursday. A Thursday meeting's MIR gives the team a structured record at the close and a clean reference in #mir-reports. Without it, the meeting still sits in the Fireflies transcript, but the agenda chain loses its input. The full cycle is in the Meeting Agendas guide.

The two types

There are two kinds of MIR, matched to the two main meeting types. The trigger phrase tells Claude which to build.

📋 Stand-up MIR after Monday stand-up

A tight summary of the Monday stand-up. Captures each person's task status, anything completed, unresolved flags, and — most importantly — the Thursday agenda items. That block is what the Thursday agenda builder reads to set the focus, so the stand-up MIR is what actually carries Monday's outcomes into Thursday.

Trigger phrases — any of these
"Run standup MIR" · "MIR for today's stand-up" · "Standup MIR" · "Generate standup MIR"
Produces
Task status per person, completions, unresolved flags, and the Thursday agenda items block
Feeds
The Thursday working meeting agenda
🗒 Work Meeting MIR after Thursday / monthly / ad hoc

A fuller report from a working session — Thursday, monthly review, or ad hoc. Captures answers to any questions asked of Claude during the meeting, every decision made, open questions, the full actions table (task, owner, deadline), risks and flags, and the focus items for the next meeting. This is the one the meeting lead reads aloud at the close.

Trigger phrases — any of these
"Run MIR" · "Run work meeting MIR" · "MIR for today's meeting" · "Generate MIR" · "Give me the MIR"
Produces
Decisions, an actions table with owners and deadlines, risks, an active scan of anything missed, and next meeting focus
Feeds
The next agenda and the log update that refreshes the project files

What's inside one

The two types contain different sections, matched to what each meeting produces. You don't write any of this — Claude builds it from the transcript — but knowing the shape helps you spot if something's missing before you confirm the send.

›Anything confirmed finished in the stand-up

›Blockers, deadlines, overdue items, risks — from the Active Scan

Why the Active Scan matters

That "active scan" line is the MIR's safety net. It means the report doesn't only capture what the meeting did — it also checks the live task list and flags what the meeting missed, then carries those items into the next meeting's focus so nothing quietly falls off the list. Both MIR types run it.

How to generate one

Every meeting gets an MIR — but when you run it differs by type. The meeting lead owns it.

Whose job this is

The meeting lead generates the MIR. If you're unsure how, Aaron can run it — or you can ask Aaron to. The important thing is that it gets done.

When to run it

Thursday and ad hoc sessions: in the last 15 minutes, as the close. The meeting lead triggers the MIR, then reads it aloud to confirm decisions and actions before anyone leaves — the close is built around the MIR.

Monday stand-up: after the meeting ends. The stand-up is a quick status check with no read-aloud close, so its MIR is generated once the meeting is done.

1

Confirm Fireflies recorded the meeting

The MIR is built entirely from the Fireflies transcript, so Fireflies has to have been in the meeting. Check this at the start of every meeting, not the end — if it isn't there, add it immediately following the Fireflies guide. If it never joined, there's no transcript and no MIR can be built.

2

Open a new chat and turn on the connectors

Start a fresh chat in the ECB project. The MIR needs two connectors on: Fireflies (to pull the transcript) and Zapier (to post to Slack). Turn them on via the + in the message box → Connectors, toggle each one on, and toggle them off again once the MIR is posted. Sonnet is fine — no need for Opus. Connector basics are in the Claude usage guide.

3

Type the trigger phrase

Use the phrase for the meeting type — Run standup MIR after a Monday stand-up, or Run MIR after a Thursday, monthly, or ad hoc session. Claude finds the transcript and builds the report.

4

Review, then confirm the send

Claude generates the MIR in the chat and shows it to you first. If anything's wrong or missing, tell Claude what to fix. Once it's right, give the okay and Claude posts it to #mir-reports.

That's the whole flow for a stand-up MIR — run it once the meeting's done. A Thursday or ad hoc close has a specific order on top of these steps, because of how Fireflies processes recordings:

The Thursday / ad hoc close sequence

The close is built around the MIR and runs in the last 15 minutes. The order matters because Fireflies has to leave a meeting before it can turn the recording into the transcript the MIR needs:

1. Re-add Fireflies for the close. Kick it out and reinvite it (per the Fireflies guide) so the close gets recorded as its own chunk. On sessions over two hours this also captures the final part Fireflies had split off.
2. Run the MIR (steps 2–4 above) and read it aloud to confirm decisions, actions, and next steps before anyone leaves.
3. Run the log update later — once the meeting has ended and Fireflies has processed the closing chunk. Run it after the meeting is fully finished, not during the close. Because the close was recorded, the log update picks up the read-aloud and confirmations too. Default is Aaron updates that night. See the Log Tracker guide.

Generating one for a missed meeting

If a meeting happened but no MIR was generated at the time, you can still produce one afterwards — as long as Fireflies recorded the meeting.

This comes up most often when a Thursday agenda has no focus items: it means the Monday stand-up's MIR was never generated, so there's nothing for Thursday to pull from. The fix is to generate the missing MIR first, then rebuild the agenda.

1

Open a new chat with Fireflies + Zapier on

Same connector setup as a normal MIR (Fireflies to pull the transcript, Zapier to post to Slack). The transcript still exists in Fireflies from when the meeting happened.

2

Trigger the MIR and name the meeting

Use the trigger phrase, but tell Claude which meeting it's for — for example, Run standup MIR for Monday's stand-up. Naming the date helps Claude find the right transcript rather than assuming it's today's.

3

Review, confirm, and let it post

Same as usual — Claude shows the MIR, you check it, confirm, and it posts to #mir-reports. The Thursday agenda can then pull the focus from it. If you're rebuilding a downstream agenda, do that after the MIR is posted.

The one limit

An MIR can only be built if Fireflies actually recorded the meeting. If Fireflies wasn't in the session, there's no transcript to build from and no MIR is possible. This is why getting Fireflies into every meeting matters — see the Fireflies guide.

Long meetings (2 hours+)

Fireflies on the free plan caps recordings at two hours, so a longer Thursday or ad hoc session gets split into multiple transcript parts — Part 1, Part 2, and so on.

This doesn't change the MIR you get. Claude pulls all parts from the same day and treats them as one meeting, producing a single report covering the whole session — never one MIR per part. If you ever suspect a part is missing, tell Claude; it should never build an MIR from a partial transcript.

The only thing you have to do is make sure every part actually got recorded. That's handled in two places, both covered by the Fireflies guide: re-adding Fireflies during the meeting when it leaves at the two-hour mark, and re-adding it again at the close before you run the MIR (step 2 above) so the final chunk — including the close itself — is captured.

If something goes wrong

Claude can't find the transcript

Check that Fireflies is turned on as a connector in this chat, and that Fireflies actually attended the meeting. If it attended but the transcript isn't showing, it may still be processing — wait a few minutes and try again. If Fireflies never joined, no MIR can be built.

The MIR didn't post to Slack

Check that the Slack connector (via Zapier) was on before you confirmed the send. If it was off, turn it on and ask Claude to post the MIR to #mir-reports. If Claude reports an error rather than posting cleanly, flag it to Aaron in #p1-important.

The MIR is missing something from the meeting

Tell Claude what's missing before you confirm the send — "you missed the decision about the merch supplier," or "the action for Ori isn't in the table." It works from the transcript, so if something was said it can be added. Don't post an incomplete MIR; fix it first.

It only captured part of a long meeting

If the meeting ran over two hours and the MIR only reflects the first part, Claude likely didn't pull every transcript part. Tell it there are more parts from the same day and to pull all of them before rebuilding.
