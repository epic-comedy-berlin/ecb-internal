Meeting Agendas

What ECB's meeting rhythm is, what each meeting is for, and how to generate an agenda and get it into Slack before a session.

Why ECB runs on meetings

A small team moving fast needs a few fixed points in the week where everyone lines up, decides things, and clears what's stuck — without constant back-and-forth in between.

That's what the meeting rhythm is for. Each meeting has a specific job, and they feed each other: what gets surfaced in one becomes the focus of the next. Claude builds every agenda from the current state of the company — open tasks, deadlines, risks, recent decisions — so each session starts from the same shared picture and nothing urgent slips through.

ECB runs two meetings a week, every week, plus a monthly review and the occasional extra session when something needs it.

What each meeting is for

Before the mechanics, the purpose. Each meeting does one job in the rhythm:

Meeting When What it's for How long
🗓Monday Stand-up Every Monday Align and surface. Quick status from each person, flag blockers, set what Thursday will focus on. No problem-solving. 15 min
🗒Thursday Working Every Thursday Decide and do. The main session — quick wins, then the priority decisions and work that move the company. 3–5 hrs
📅Monthly Review Last Thursday of month Review and clear. Clean up the task list, promote and cull, agree one process improvement. Replaces that week's working session. 3–5 hrs
⚡Ad Hoc As needed Overflow or urgency. A focused extra session for something that can't wait, or when there's more to work through than a Thursday holds. 2–5 hrs
The loop

The meetings aren't standalone — they form a cycle. Monday sets Thursday's focus. Every meeting ends with a written report (an MIR) that records what happened, and that report feeds the next agenda. The full loop is covered further down in How it all connects.

How to generate one

Same process for all four types. The difference is the meeting type and how much detail you give up front.

Whose job this is

The meeting lead generates the agenda for their meeting. If you're unsure how, Aaron can generate it — or you can ask Aaron to build it for you. Better to ask than to send a broken or half-built agenda.

1

Open a new chat in the ECB project

Start a fresh chat. One agenda per chat — don't reuse an old one.

2

Check connectors and model

The build needs Slack (via Zapier) on to post, and may use Fireflies for the Thursday and ad hoc types. Turn the needed ones on, and off again after. Sonnet is fine — you don't need Opus.

3

Type the request with details up front

Give the meeting type plus the key details in one message — type, start time, and (for Thursday, monthly, ad hoc) duration. For an ad hoc session, include the focus topic. The more you give, the fewer questions Claude has to ask. Exact phrasing and examples are in the Meeting type reference below.

4

Answer anything still needed

If you left something out, Claude asks — answer or accept the defaults. For Thursday, don't set the focus; it comes from Monday's MIR automatically. You can add extra items here too: "also put the merch decision on the agenda."

5

Review, then confirm the send

Claude generates the full agenda in the chat and shows it to you first. Read it over — it's built from the live files, but a quick check confirms nothing looks off. If something's wrong, tell Claude what to change. Once you're happy, confirm and it posts the agenda to #meeting-agendas, ready for the meeting lead to run from.

Push back any time

You can correct Claude during the build — "that task is already done," "the focus should be X instead," "add the SociableKIT deadline." It adjusts. The agenda isn't locked until it's posted.

Meeting type reference

The detail for each type — trigger phrases, the best way to ask, and what Claude pulls from. Use this when you're actually building one.

🗓 Monday Stand-up 15 min · weekly

A tight 15-minute status check. Each person runs through what they completed, what they're working on, and what's blocking them. No problem-solving — anything needing discussion is parked for Thursday. Claude pre-populates flags so the meeting lead can raise them directly rather than waiting for the team to surface them.

🗒 Thursday Working Meeting 3–5 hours · weekly

The main working session of the week. Opens with quick wins and a task run-through, then moves into the priority agenda — the decisions and discussions that move the company. Focus items come automatically from the most recent Monday stand-up's MIR, so Thursday picks up exactly where Monday left off.

📅 Monthly Review 3–5 hours · monthly

Replaces the last Thursday working meeting of the month. A review and clean-up of the whole task list — the team clears overdue and flagged items, promotes the right P2 tasks into P1, culls stale P3/P4 tasks, and agrees one process improvement for next month. It runs long (3–5 hours) because going through the full task list properly is a big job. Keeps the list from silting up.

An extra session outside the normal cadence. Called when something urgent can't wait until Thursday, or when there's more to work through than a single Thursday can hold and the team needs additional time for specific tasks. Built around one or two named focus items — not a general catch-up. You provide the focus; Claude builds the session around it and surfaces related flags so nothing gets missed.

Every meeting ends with an MIR

The agenda gets you into the meeting. The MIR is how the meeting gets captured — and it doesn't happen automatically. Someone has to generate it.

At the close of every meeting, the meeting lead triggers a Meeting Intelligence Report. Claude pulls the meeting's Fireflies transcript, builds the MIR, and posts it to #mir-reports. This is the step that records every decision, task, and flag from the session — and it's the input the next agenda and the project files depend on. See the MIR guide for that process, and the Slack guide for the channels.

Don't skip this

If the MIR isn't generated, the meeting effectively vanishes from the system. The Thursday agenda won't have its focus, decisions won't reach the project files, and the next log update has nothing to pull. Generating the MIR at close is not optional. See the MIR guide for exactly how.

Then the log update

After the MIR is posted, the project files get refreshed with a log update — which scans the MIR, Slack, and transcripts and folds everything into Active State and the Decision Archive. The order matters: MIR first, then log update. Running the log update before the MIR means it misses the meeting's own output. This happens after every meeting, not just Thursdays. See the Log Tracker guide for how.

How it all connects

The agenda system is one link in a loop that keeps the company in sync. Each meeting's output becomes the next meeting's input.

Monday feeds Thursday. When the Monday stand-up wraps and its MIR is generated, the MIR captures a "Thursday agenda items" block. The Thursday agenda pulls directly from that — so what gets parked on Monday automatically becomes Thursday's focus. You don't manually carry anything over.

The cycle

Monday stand-up → MIR (records Thursday focus) → log update → Thursday agenda → Thursday MIR → log update → next Monday's agenda. Every meeting produces an MIR, and every MIR is followed by a log update that keeps the files current for the next agenda.

If something goes wrong

The agenda didn't post to Slack

Check that the Slack connector (via Zapier) was turned on before generating. If it was off, turn it on and ask Claude to send the agenda to #meeting-agendas. If Claude reports an error sending rather than posting cleanly, flag it to Aaron in #p1-important.

The Thursday agenda has no focus items

This usually means there's no recent Monday MIR for Claude to pull from — either the stand-up didn't happen or its MIR wasn't generated. If the stand-up happened but the MIR was missed, go and generate the MIR for that meeting first, then rebuild the Thursday agenda — it'll pick up the focus. The MIR guide covers how to generate one for a past meeting. If the stand-up genuinely didn't happen, just tell Claude what the focus should be when it asks.

The agenda looks out of date

The agenda is only as current as the project files. If tasks or decisions look stale, the files need a log update before the agenda will be accurate. Normally Aaron runs this — flag it to him in #p1-important. If he's unavailable and it's essential, you can run the log update yourself and replace the files in the project. The Log Tracker guide walks through how.
